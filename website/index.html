Made for the way you explain

Everything you need.
Nothing in your way.

A practical writing companion for lessons, live explanations, meetings and study sessions.

Live page mirroring

Your page background and pen strokes appear inside the Windows companion as you write.

Simple Wi-Fi connection

Connect both devices to the same Wi-Fi or hotspot. Enter the laptop address and six-digit code—no Bluetooth pairing or COM ports.

PDF annotation

Open an existing PDF, write on its pages, move between pages and export a shareable annotated copy.

Notebook templates

Start with blank, ruled, grid, dot-grid or Cornell pages. Build a notebook with up to 100 pages.

Immersive tablet mode

Hide Android's status and navigation bars for a clean writing area. Swipe from the edge to reveal them temporarily.

Optional desktop overlay

Keep the in-app canvas visible, or show only the ink over PowerPoint, a PDF reader or another desktop app.

Flexible tool dock

Move the icon toolbar to either side of the tablet, or collapse it when you need more writing space.

Two-way file sharing

Send images and files between Android and Windows over your local connection without cloud uploads.

Quick page capture

Save the full writing page or drag-select a crop directly from the Windows preview as a PNG.

Up and writing in minutes

Three steps.
One connected workspace.

No accounts, drivers or complicated pairing workflow. Just your devices and a shared local network.

01

Open the Windows app

Connect your laptop to Wi-Fi. InkBridge shows its local IP address and a six-digit connection code.

02

Connect your tablet

Join the same Wi-Fi network, enter the laptop IP and code in InkBridge, then connect.

03

Write and explain

Choose a tool, open a PDF or notebook, and write. Your page and strokes show up on the PC.

Both devices need to be on the same reachable local network. Some guest or campus Wi-Fi networks block device-to-device traffic. A private mobile hotspot is a good alternative.

Good to know

A few things
before you begin.

Does InkBridge use Bluetooth?
The current 0.3 release uses local Wi-Fi (TCP) for writing. Bluetooth pairing and COM-port setup are not required.
Do both devices need internet access?
No. They need to be on the same reachable local Wi-Fi network or hotspot. Internet is only needed to check for and download app updates.
Does it mirror the entire tablet screen?
No. InkBridge sends the writing page, its background or PDF page, and the ink strokes. Your Android status bar and unrelated apps are not mirrored.
Can I write on existing PDFs?
Yes. Open a PDF on Android, annotate its pages, navigate through the document and export an annotated copy. Exported pages are flattened images, so searchable PDF text is not preserved.
What if my Wi-Fi blocks the connection?
Some guest or managed campus networks isolate devices. Try a private mobile hotspot and allow InkBridge through Windows Firewall on Private networks.
Is the local connection encrypted?
The six-digit code gates access to the local TCP session, but the ink stream is not end-to-end encrypted. Use a trusted private network and avoid untrusted public Wi-Fi for sensitive material.
Which devices are supported?
Android 8.0 or later and Windows 10/11 x64. An active stylus is recommended, but finger input is also supported.
